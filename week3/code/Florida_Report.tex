\begin{center}
    \Large \textbf{Is Florida Getting Warmer?}\\[0.3cm]
    \normalsize Key West Annual Mean Temperature, 1900–2000
\end{center}

\vspace{0.5cm}

\noindent\textbf{Objective:} To test whether the mean annual temperature in Key West, Florida, has increased significantly during the 20\textsuperscript{th} century. Because successive annual temperatures are not independent, we used a permutation test rather than the standard correlation test.

\vspace{0.3cm}

\noindent\textbf{Methods:} 
We calculated the Pearson correlation coefficient between Year and Temperature. 
To assess its significance, we generated 10{,}000 random permutations of temperature values using R's \texttt{sample()} function, recalculated the correlation for each, and computed the fraction of random correlations greater than the observed one (the approximate one-sided p-value).

\vspace{0.3cm}

\noindent\textbf{Results:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Observed correlation between Year and Temperature: \textbf{r = 0.53}
    \item One-sided permutation p-value: \textbf{p = 0.0031}
\end{itemize}

\noindent These results indicate a statistically significant positive trend: average annual temperature in Key West has increased over the 20\textsuperscript{th} century.

\noindent\textbf{Interpretation:}  
The observed correlation (r = 0.53) lies far to the right of the null distribution generated by permutation, and fewer than 0.5\% of random correlations were as large or larger than the observed one.  
This suggests a strong warming trend in Key West during the 20\textsuperscript{th} century.
